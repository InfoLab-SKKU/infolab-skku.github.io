# {% include icon.html icon="fa-solid fa-door-open" %}Join Us

Between new research projects and graduating lab members, we are frequently recruiting motivated people at every career stage.

## What We Look For

- A solid foundation in **computer science, systems, or applied mathematics** — though we are open to candidates with unconventional academic or professional backgrounds.
- Programming proficiency — Python and a deep-learning framework such as PyTorch is a plus, and systems or security tooling experience is valued for security topics.
- Curiosity about real-world problems in **AI security, adversarial ML, medical AI, or explainable AI**, and the persistence to chase them through experiments.
- Clear written communication — research is reading, writing, and arguing with evidence as much as it is coding.

## What You Can Expect

- Active projects spanning [security & adversarial ML, biomedical AI, and explainable AI](/projects/) with publications at venues such as NDSS, ACM KDD, IEEE TDSC, IEEE TIFS, and Information Fusion.
- Collaboration with academic and industry partners in Korea and internationally — see our [collaborators](/collaborators/).
- Mentoring toward your own first-author publications, starting from your first semester.
- A diverse, international lab — meet the [current team](/team/).

## How to Apply

1. **Read about our research.** Browse our [projects](/projects/) and [recent publications](/pubs/) and identify where your interests fit.
2. **Email us through the [contact page](/contact/).** Attach your CV (with GPA and transcripts for student applicants), links to your GitHub or publications if available, and a short statement of what you want to work on.
3. **Graduate applicants:** also apply through SKKU's official [Graduate School admissions](https://admission-global.skku.edu/eng/index.html), and mention your interest in InfoLab and Prof. Abuhmed's research in your application.

**Important:** if your message does not clearly explain *why* you are interested in InfoLab specifically, it may be overlooked. Personalized, thoughtful inquiries that reference our papers or projects are strongly encouraged — generic mass emails are not.
